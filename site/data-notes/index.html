Data notes and limitations

The dataset is synthetic. It is useful for demonstrating an analytics workflow without exposing any customer, employee, or company information.

What the sample represents

The generator creates daily request volumes for four regions and three user profiles from January through September 2026. It includes weekday effects, a repeating demand wave, gradual growth, random noise, occasional spikes, staffing variation, backlog, SLA performance, quality errors, overtime, and average resolution time.

What it does not represent

The numbers are not Nokia data and are not estimates of Nokia operations. They do not represent a real support organization, customer base, staffing plan, or business forecast.

Forecast interpretation

The 14-day forecast is a baseline for planning exercises. It does not include holidays, promotions, outages, product releases, weather, business calendars, or external demand drivers. Those would be reasonable additions for a real deployment.

Staffing interpretation

The staffing calculation assumes that recent productivity is a useful short-term baseline and that planning for 85 percent utilization leaves room for normal variation. A real staffing model would also account for skills, schedules, shrinkage, queue priorities, training, and service-level targets.

Why keep the sample reproducible?

The random generator uses a fixed seed. That means the analysis can be rerun and reviewed without the underlying dataset changing unexpectedly. It also makes automated tests meaningful.

Synthetic data keeps the project safe to share while preserving the shape of a real analytics workflow.
